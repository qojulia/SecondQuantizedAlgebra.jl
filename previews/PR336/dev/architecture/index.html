Architecture

This page is for anyone who changes code under src/ or ext/. It describes how the package is put together and lists the invariants a change must keep. The invariants matter most: many of them look like missed optimizations or arbitrary choices, and several are not covered by any test.

Overview

An expression flows through four layers.

constructors ──► Op ──*,+──► QAdd  (canonical polynomial, Dict{QTerm, Coeff})
                                │
                 sin, cos, expim│                     taylor(expr, 0:n)
                                ▼                            │
                              QExpr (formal, ordered tree) ──┘ back to QAdd
                                │
                    average     ▼          to_numeric / numeric_average
             QAdd ──────► symbolic scalars           QAdd ──────► backend operator
                          (SymbolicUtils terms)                   (package extension)

Every operator is the single concrete struct Op, and its role (annihilation, transition, Pauli, …) is a kind tag. A polynomial expression is a QAdd: a dictionary from terms to coefficients, where a term is an ordered operator product together with the index inequalities that scope it. Products and sums are brought into canonical form the moment they are built. Coefficients are Coeff values: exact numbers, parameter polynomials, or raw SymbolicUtils expressions. Formal operator functions live in a separate tree, QExpr, which never enters the polynomial code. Averages turn a QAdd into SymbolicUtils terms for equation-of-motion packages such as QuantumCumulants.jl, and the numeric layer converts a QAdd to matrices through a backend defined in a package extension. Maps on operators, SuperOperator, Lindbladian and SuperExpr, form a parallel layer whose two sides are canonical operator products.

Invariants

Each invariant states the rule, the reason, and the test that pins it, or "not tested" where a break would go unnoticed.

Canonical form

Every product is canonical when it is built. The result of * is already sorted, reduced and commuted, so normal_order and simplify are idempotent on it and like terms collect at once. A new code path that builds terms must end in the canonicalization pipeline. Pinned by test/algebra/.

Rewrites come in three classes. Local identities on a provably shared site (transition composition, Pauli products) always fire. Commutation swaps fire only when the site relation is known. Rewrites that multiply the number of terms, such as completeness, fire only on request. This is why the site comparison distinguishes Equal from Undetermined, and why it must keep doing so.

The pipeline runs reduce, then commute, then reduce. The first reduce composes transitions and Pauli operators, which compose rather than commute. Commutation then handles bosonic, spin and quadrature pairs, and the final reduce catches compositions that a commutator residual creates next to a neighbour.

The ground-state projector $\sigma^{gg}$ stays atomic. Rewriting it as $1 - \sum_{k \neq g} \sigma^{kk}$ multiplies a product with $k$ such factors into $n^k$ terms before anything can cancel, and a mean-field solver would then carry $n - 1$ dependent moments instead of one. expand_completeness is the explicit handle. Pinned by test/algebra/simplify_test.jl.

Free indices stay undetermined. Two operators on the same space with different free indices may or may not act on the same site, so no rule fires between them and they keep their written order. Only a Σ or assume_distinct_index resolves them. Pinned by test/fundamentals/indexing_test.jl.

The canonical form does not depend on declaration order. Operator and index names are interned to integer ids in insertion order, so ordering by id would make the canonical form depend on which name was declared first. All orderings go through the alphabetical rank of the name instead. Not tested.

A pass that forks must copy. The pipeline streams terms through nested passes without materializing intermediate lists. A pass with one output may mutate the operator vector in place; a pass with several outputs copies it for every branch but one. A missed copy gives two terms that share one vector, a silent aliasing bug. Not tested.

Diagonal splitting substitutes before sorting. When a product meets a summed index, the term splits into the off-diagonal part and the part where the two indices coincide. The coinciding part substitutes the index in the unsorted product: sorting first can put two same-site operators in the wrong physical order, so that $\sigma_j^{12}\sigma_i^{21}$ at $i = j$ would give $\sigma^{22}$ instead of $\sigma^{11}$. Pinned by test/fundamentals/indexing_test.jl.

Every coincidence is counted once. A summed index that may equal several free indices takes the first one it equals, so the diagonal for the $k$-th excludes the earlier ones. Several binders are split one at a time, each inside the parts of the previous one; splitting each against the original term would count a joint coincidence such as $i = k, j = l$ once per binder. Pinned by test/symbolic/index_cancellation_test.jl.

Terms and indices

A term is its operators together with its inequalities. Like terms merge only when both the operator product and the inequality constraints match, so $\sum_i a_i$ and $\sum_{i \neq j} a_i$ remain separate entries. Zero coefficients are never stored. Pinned by test/fundamentals/indexing_test.jl.

Every stored inequality refers to something the term can see. A constraint must involve an index of one of the term's operators, of its coefficient, or of the enclosing sum. The QAdd constructor drops the others, since they would only prevent like terms from merging. Pinned by test/symbolic/index_cancellation_test.jl.

The algebra never creates an Index. Every index in an output was constructed by the user, so the user can match on it, substitute it and give it limits. Hence a product of two sums over the same bound index throws instead of renaming one of them, and $\sum_i \sigma_i + \sum_j \sigma_j$ stays two terms. Pinned by test/fundamentals/indexing_test.jl.

Binary + copies; reductions do not. Both operands of + are still held by the caller, so + copies the accumulated dictionary and a chain of $n$ additions costs $O(n^2)$. sum and reduce(+, …) over an array instead thread one accumulator through the fold and build the result once; the result must stay identical to the chain of +. Products are left as repeated *, because their cost is the distributive expansion itself, which an accumulator cannot save. Pinned by test/algebra/algebra_test.jl.

Interned ids never leave the session. The name and range tables in intern.jl are written only at construction, from one thread, and their ids follow insertion order. Ids are never serialized, and no SymbolicUtils symbol built from them may be cached at precompile time: it would be a stale copy of the symbol the new session hashconses. Not tested.

Expression layers

QExpr never touches the polynomial path. Polynomial work always stays in canonical QAdd leaves, and a formal function is never expanded implicitly. taylor is the only way back to QAdd, and average, to_numeric and numeric_average refuse an unlowered QExpr. A change to QExpr must not cost the polynomial algebra anything. Pinned by test/algebra/operator_function_*.

Superoperators

Maps are not QFields. Were SuperOperator or Lindbladian a QField, every operator method would accept them, and a * L or average(L) would return nonsense instead of an error. The three map types share the internal Union AnyMap, since AbstractSuperOperator belongs to QuantumInterface. Pinned by test/numeric/superoperator_refusal_test.jl.

Both sides share one scope. In $\sum_i \sigma_i \rho \sigma_i^\dagger$ the same $i$ binds both sides, so a term stores one inequality list and one summation scope for the pair. The diagonal split runs on the joint unsorted term, and every binder the result no longer mentions is counted, repeatedly, since eliminating one can make another's count known. Without this, transpose(Σ(dissipator(a_i'), i))(1) would be $1 - N$ instead of zero. Pinned by test/symbolic/superoperator_indexed_test.jl.

Ambiguous indices throw. Composing maps that bind the same index, applying a map to an operator with that index free, or a side carrying its own Σ has no unique reading, and the algebra never mints an index to choose one. Pinned by test/symbolic/superoperator_indexed_test.jl.

Lindbladian equality is structural. Normalization moves jump constants into the Hamiltonian, numeric factors and unimodular phases into the rate, and drops the identity part of $H$, so equal spellings of one generator compare equal. A cross term is not expanded: dissipator(a + b) and its four-channel expansion are unequal as Lindbladians and equal as SuperOperators, which keeps the user's channels through printing and frames. σᵍᵍ stays atomic here as well. Pinned by test/algebra/lindbladian_test.jl and test/algebra/superoperator_structural_test.jl.

GKSL extraction is exact by default. Float maps carry roundoff that grows as the basis approaches linear dependence, so no fixed tolerance both keeps small genuine terms and accepts every valid float map; rtol is an opt-in, measured in map units so that rescaling the basis cannot hide a term. Trace preservation is checked first, so a trace defect is reported as one. Pinned by test/algebra/gksl_coordinates_test.jl.

Channel order is reproducible. channels(L) sorts by channel_order_key, since Dict order depends on hashing and printing and positional access must not. Pinned by test/algebra/gksl_coordinates_test.jl.

The return type never depends on values. A side with a formal function makes a SuperExpr, and a SuperExpr whose formal part is empty stays one until SuperOperator(X) is called. Pinned by @inferred checks in test/algebra/lindbladian_test.jl.

Coefficients

Complex{Num} exists only at the boundary. Coefficients are Coeff internally; Complex{Num} appears only where values leave the package, as in get_prefactor or to_num. In particular, average must not build a Complex{Num}: Symbolics turns it into an opaque complex(re, im) node that simplify cannot cancel and that generated code cannot call. Pinned by test/symbolic/average_test.jl.

Exactness is a type. An exact value is a GaussianRational, and a ComplexF64 is always inexact. Exact with exact stays exact, and anything combined with a float becomes a float, so no operation has to inspect a value. Pinned by test/symbolic/coefficients_test.jl.

One tier per value. Exact arithmetic runs on Int and is redone on BigInt when it overflows, so its steps must not mutate their inputs. A result returns to Int when it fits, so the same value never exists in both tiers. The redo is not tested for mutation.

Radicals are canonical. A radical is a product of prime square roots (or other roots) with exponents in $(0, 1)$, so $\sqrt{6}$, $\sqrt{2}\sqrt{3}$ and $\sqrt{24}/2$ are the same value with the same hash. Pinned by test/symbolic/coefficients_test.jl.

Only plain products become compound atoms. A function of an atom, or of a product of real atoms such as cos(ω*t), is a polynomial atom, which keeps its arithmetic on the fast path. Scaled or summed arguments stay raw, since sin(2θ) must reach the trigonometric reduction and exp(-r) must cancel 1/exp(r). Pinned by test/symbolic/reduction_test.jl and test/algebra/unitary_transformations_test.jl.

GaussianRational is not a Number. Methods of a new Number subtype on ==, hash and arithmetic invalidate SymbolicUtils code compiled for abstract numbers, which slowed its simplify by about a fifth. Not tested.

Averages

An average is real when its operator is Hermitian. The average and sum nodes override maketerm to derive their type from the operator, since the default rebuild in substitute would silently turn a real average back into a complex one. The index scope of a sum node is an argument, not metadata, because SymbolicUtils ignores metadata in isequal and two sums with different scopes would otherwise cancel. Pinned by test/symbolic/average_test.jl.

Orderings

Three orderings, three purposes. The site comparison decides which neighbours may swap and is deliberately partial. The display order in sorted_arguments is total but may tie distinct operators. Only order_key is faithful to identity, order_key(a) == order_key(b) exactly when isequal(a, b), and downstream packages use it to choose canonical representatives. None of them is hash, which is not stable across Julia versions. Pinned by test/fundamentals/ordering_test.jl.

Unitary transformations

The affine action is the single representation. A UnitaryTransform stores $X \mapsto L X + c$ as blocks, one per algebra: bosonic, phase space, spin and Pauli, finite-level. Each block is inverted with the formula of its own group, so inv never inverts a symbolic matrix, and the rewrite rules are compiled from the action. Caller-supplied matrices are checked for structure but never for unitarity, so no tolerance or projection is applied. Pinned by test/algebra/unitary_*.

Types and performance

Struct fields are concrete, and Op and Index are bits types. Vector{Op} is then an inline buffer that is hashed and compared on integers. Pinned by test/quality/quality_test.jl.

The numeric core is backend-neutral. src/numeric/ never names a backend type, and the type of an assembled operator does not depend on the number of terms. Pinned by test/numeric/backend_interface_test.jl and JET.
