Open Systems API

SecondQuantizedAlgebra.Lindbladian — Type
Lindbladian{O}

A generator in GKSL form with its structure retained,

L = -i[H, ·] + Σ γ Σ_scope 𝒟[J, K],

with O = QAdd for polynomial models and O = QExpr when the Hamiltonian or a jump is a formal operator function. Build it with hamiltonian_action, dissipator and liouvillian, and combine with +, scalar * and Σ.

Lindbladian is a form, not a physicality claim: H may be non-Hermitian and rates may be complex or negative. Every Lindbladian is trace preserving, transpose(L)(1) == 0. It preserves Hermiticity when H is Hermitian and the rates are Hermitian over channel pairs, and it generates a completely positive semigroup when, in addition, every rate matrix is positive semidefinite.

Composition, transpose, adjoint and application lower L to a SuperOperator (or a SuperExpr for formal models). Equality is structural; compare maps with SuperOperator(L₁) == SuperOperator(L₂).

SecondQuantizedAlgebra.dissipator — Function
dissipator(J) -> Lindbladian
dissipator(J, K) -> Lindbladian
dissipator(J, Γ, i => j, ...) -> Lindbladian

dissipator(J) is 𝒟[J](ρ) = J ρ J' - (J'J ρ + ρ J'J)/2, and dissipator(J, K) the cross term 𝒟[J, K](ρ) = J ρ K' - (K'J ρ + ρ K'J)/2. Write rates as scalar prefactors, κ * dissipator(a).

dissipator(J, Γ, i => j) is the correlated family Σᵢ Σⱼ Γ 𝒟[Jᵢ, Jⱼ], where i is a free index of J and j a fresh index on the same space and range, chosen by the caller. The diagonal i = j is split off as Γ(i, i) 𝒟[Jᵢ]. Several pairs give a multi-index family.

SecondQuantizedAlgebra.liouvillian — Function
liouvillian(H, J; rates = nothing) -> Lindbladian

The generator -i[H, ·] + Σᵢⱼ Γ[i, j] 𝒟[J[i], J[j]] in the QuantumOpticsBase convention. rates is nothing (all ones), a vector of per-channel rates, or a matrix Γ whose entry Γ[i, j] multiplies J[i] ρ J[j]'. An empty J gives the coherent part only.

SecondQuantizedAlgebra.SuperOperator — Type
SuperOperator

A polynomial linear map on operators, ρ ↦ Σ c * L * ρ * R, stored as a dictionary of SuperTerm sandwiches with their coefficients, plus a summation scope indices shared by both sides and the coefficient.

Build maps with spre, spost, sprepost, or by lowering a Lindbladian. Maps compose with * or ∘ (the right factor acts first), apply to operators by calling them, and support transpose (the Heisenberg-picture map) and the Hilbert–Schmidt adjoint. Iteration yields Pair{SuperTerm, CNum}.

SuperOperator is not a QField: a map is not an operator, so it never enters operator products or averages by accident.

SuperOperator(pairs, indices)

Rebuild a map from SuperTerm => coefficient pairs and a summation scope. Every sandwich is canonicalized and unused constraints are pruned.

source
SecondQuantizedAlgebra.SuperExpr — Type
SuperExpr

A superoperator with formal operator-function sides, poly + Σ c * f ρ g. The polynomial part is a SuperOperator; every sandwich with a QExpr side is a FormalSandwich with its scalar moved into the coefficient. Sides are split into their additive atoms, so c * (f, g), (c f, g) and (f, c g) are one key, and a sandwich whose sides are both polynomial lands in the polynomial part.

Operations keep the SuperExpr type even when the formal part is empty; call SuperOperator(X) to convert, or taylor(X, 0:n) to lower every formal side.

SecondQuantizedAlgebra.SuperTerm — Type
SuperTerm

One sandwich ρ ↦ left * ρ * right of a SuperOperator, with the pairwise index inequality constraints shared by both sides.

Fields

  • left::Vector{Op}: the ordered operator product acting from the left
  • right::Vector{Op}: the ordered operator product acting from the right
  • ne::Vector{NonEqualPair}: pairwise α ≠ β constraints (canonicalized)

SuperTerm is the dict key of a SuperOperator; iterating one yields Pair{SuperTerm, CNum}. The fields are owned by the stored key and must not be mutated. Build keys with superterm.

SecondQuantizedAlgebra.Channel — Type
Channel{O}

The structured dissipator Σ_scope 𝒟[jump, partner] of a Lindbladian, with 𝒟[J, K](ρ) = J ρ K' - (K'J ρ + ρ K'J)/2. The rate is stored as the channel's value in the Lindbladian, not in the key. A diagonal channel stores the jump as its own partner. Read the fields with jump, partner and scope.

Channels are normalized on construction: constants are stripped from the jumps and folded into the Hamiltonian, native numeric content of the jumps moves into the rate, jumps carry no bound sums, and a summation index the channel does not mention is counted into the rate.

GKSL coordinates

SecondQuantizedAlgebra.JumpBasis — Type
JumpBasis(operators...)
JumpBasis(operators::AbstractVector)

An ordered basis of jump operators $(F_1, …, F_q)$ in which a map is written in GKSL form,

L(ρ) = -i[H, ρ] + Σ_{μν} d_{μν} (F_μ ρ F_ν' - {F_ν' F_μ, ρ}/2),

with d the Kossakowski matrix returned by kossakowski. Each operator is canonicalized with expand_completeness and taken modulo the identity, since a scalar shift of a jump changes only the Hamiltonian. The operators must be linearly independent modulo the identity, but they need not be orthogonal, normalized or complete. Their order fixes the order of d, so bases with the same span in a different order are different. An empty basis is the basis of a coherent map.

A JumpBasis iterates and indexes like a vector of its canonical operators.

julia> @qnumbers a::Destroy(FockSpace(:c));julia> JumpBasis(a + 2, a' * a)JumpBasis with 2 operators:  1: a  2: a' * a

See also independent_basis, support_basis.

SecondQuantizedAlgebra.kossakowski — Function
kossakowski(S::SuperOperator, basis::JumpBasis; rtol = 0) -> Matrix{CNum}
kossakowski(L::Lindbladian, basis::JumpBasis; rtol = 0) -> Matrix{CNum}

The Hermitian Kossakowski matrix d of a map in the ordered basis:

S(ρ) = -i[H, ρ] + Σ_{μν} d_{μν} (F_μ ρ F_ν' - {F_ν' F_μ, ρ}/2).

The extraction works in the operator algebra and introduces no Hilbert-space cutoff. It throws an ArgumentError when the map has a jump direction outside basis, and a GKSLFormError when the map is not of GKSL form. A Lindbladian is read through its SuperOperator, so its own channels need not lie in basis.

The extraction is exact. Floating-point coefficients carry the roundoff of building the map, so a float map is usually of GKSL form only up to that roundoff and is rejected; write rates as exact rationals such as 1//10 or as symbolic parameters instead. A positive rtol opts into a heuristic: a leftover float coefficient counts as roundoff when, for every parameter monomial, it is at most rtol times the size of the coefficients it was computed from. No single rtol suits every basis, since roundoff grows as the basis operators approach linear dependence, and genuine terms below rtol are dropped. Exact coefficients are always compared exactly.

julia> @qnumbers a::Destroy(FockSpace(:c));julia> using Symbolics: @variablesjulia> @variables κ::Real γ::Real;julia> kossakowski(κ * dissipator(a) + γ * dissipator(a' * a), JumpBasis(a, a + a' * a))2×2 Matrix{SecondQuantizedAlgebra.Coeff}: γ + κ  -γ -γ     γ

See also Lindbladian, support_basis.

SecondQuantizedAlgebra.Lindbladian — Method
Lindbladian(S::SuperOperator[, basis::JumpBasis]; rtol = 0)

Read a map in GKSL form back as a structured generator. The result has the Hamiltonian of S and one channel d_{μν} 𝒟[F_μ, F_ν] for every nonzero entry of the Kossakowski matrix in basis, so SuperOperator(Lindbladian(S, basis)) equals S up to completeness expansion (and up to the terms a positive rtol drops). Without a basis the support_basis of S is used. The Hamiltonian is defined modulo the identity, which hamiltonian_action drops.

Throws and handles floating-point coefficients like kossakowski. The rates are not checked for positivity.

julia> @qnumbers a::Destroy(FockSpace(:c));julia> using SecondQuantizedAlgebra: SuperOperatorjulia> Lindbladian(SuperOperator(dissipator(a + 3)))-i[3//2im * a + -3//2im * a', ·] + 𝒟[a]

SecondQuantizedAlgebra.to_numeric_master — Function
to_numeric_master(L::Lindbladian, h, dims; backend, parameter, time_parameter, sites, tol)
    -> (hamiltonian = H, collapse_operators = c_ops)

Convert a Lindbladian to the inputs of a master-equation solver, so that H, c_ops = to_numeric_master(L, h, dims) feeds QuantumOptics timeevolution.master and QuantumToolbox mesolve without rate arguments.

Channels are unrolled over their scopes (bound indices need sites, as in the indexed to_numeric form), and scalar_subs resolves coefficients of unrolled indices, such as g(k). The jumps are expanded in their operator monomials M_m, and the rate matrix Γ[m, n] of every coupled block is checked to be Hermitian and positive semidefinite and diagonalized, c_k = √λ_k Σ_m U[m, k] M_m; a single channel gives √γ J. Eigenvalues with |λ| ≤ tol * max|λ| are clipped to zero (tol = √eps by default), and a more negative eigenvalue or a non-Hermitian H throws. A time-dependent H is checked at a few sample times; with (p, t) coefficients it is checked symbolically, treating parameters as real.

With a time_parameter (QuantumToolbox only), H becomes a QobjEvo and a diagonal channel with a time-dependent rate γ(t) gives the collapse operator √γ(t) J. Such a rate must stay real and nonnegative: it is checked at the sample times, and evaluating a negative or complex rate during the solve throws. A correlated channel with a time-dependent rate is refused, because the eigenvectors of Γ(t) move with t; convert the full generator with to_numeric(L, ...; time_parameter) instead.
